# B31.8S and B31G: Pipeline Integrity

Two companion standards that support the pipeline sections. Neither replaces B31.4 or B31.8; they tell an operator how to keep an existing line safe.

## B31.8S Managing System Integrity of Gas Pipelines

**Free PDF:** ASME B31.8S-2004 ([open PDF](../pdfs/asme.b31.8s.2004.pdf), 8 MB · [archive.org page](https://archive.org/details/gov.law.asme.b31.8s.2004)), incorporated by reference in 49 CFR 192 Subpart O. Current edition: 2025 ([ASME](https://www.asme.org/codes-standards/find-codes-standards/b31-8s-managing-system-integrity-gas-pipelines)).

B31.8S applies to onshore gas pipelines built under B31.8 and describes an **integrity management program**: identify threats, gather data, assess risk, inspect, repair, and measure how well the program works.

### Key ideas

**Threats.** Incident data were sorted into 22 root causes. Setting aside "unknown", B31.8S groups the remaining **21 threats** into **nine categories** under three time-related types (para. 2.2):

| Type | Categories |
| --- | --- |
| Time-dependent | External corrosion; internal corrosion; stress corrosion cracking |
| Stable | Manufacturing-related defects; welding and fabrication-related; equipment |
| Time-independent | Third-party or mechanical damage; incorrect operational procedure; weather-related and outside force |

Time-dependent threats grow, so they need periodic reassessment. Stable threats stay put unless something such as a pressure increase activates them. Time-independent threats can occur at any moment.

**Prescriptive or performance-based.** A prescriptive program follows the fixed data requirements and intervals in the standard (Appendix A, Table 3). A performance-based program uses more data and more detailed risk analysis to justify its own choices.

**Potential impact radius.** For natural gas, section 3 estimates the radius of the area a rupture could affect as

\[ r = 0.69 \, d \, \sqrt{p} \]

with \(r\) in feet, \(d\) the outside diameter in inches and \(p\) the maximum allowable operating pressure in psig. For a 30-in. line at 1,000 psig, \(r \approx 0.69 \times 30 \times 31.6 \approx 655\) ft.

**Three assessment methods.** In-line inspection ("pigging"), pressure testing and direct assessment (Appendix B). The method must suit the threat: a pressure test finds defects that fail at test pressure, while ILI tools find and size metal loss, dents or cracks depending on the tool.

## B31G Remaining Strength of Corroded Pipelines

**Free PDF:** ASME B31G-2012 ([open PDF](../pdfs/asme.b31g.2012.pdf), 39 MB · [archive.org page](https://archive.org/details/pub.cfr.asme.b31g.2012)), incorporated by reference in 10 CFR 851.27. Current edition: 2023.

B31G answers one question: **can a corroded pipe stay in service at its current pressure, and if not, at what pressure?** It applies to pipelines under B31.4, B31.8, B31.11 and B31.12 Part PL (para. 1.1). The 2012 edition offers four levels of evaluation, from a table lookup to a finite element analysis (para. 1.6).

### Key ideas

1. **Measure** the maximum depth \(d\) and the longitudinal extent \(L\) of the corroded area (Fig. 2.1-1), and compare depth with the wall thickness \(t\).
2. **Level 0: look it up (section 3).** Metal loss no deeper than 10% of nominal wall is not limited in length; deeper than 80% cannot be evaluated with the tables. In between, the table for the pipe size gives the allowable length; the area is acceptable if its measured length does not exceed it. The tables were built from the original B31G equations and give a safety factor of at least 1.39 for pipe at 72% of SMYS (para. 2.1).
3. **Level 1: calculate the failure stress \(S_F\) (para. 2.2).** With \(z = L^2/(Dt)\), the original B31G method is

    \[ M = \sqrt{1 + 0.8z}, \qquad S_F = S_{flow} \left[ \frac{1 - \tfrac{2}{3}(d/t)}{1 - \tfrac{2}{3}(d/t)/M} \right] \quad (z \le 20) \]

    and \(S_F = S_{flow}(1 - d/t)\) for \(z > 20\), with flow stress \(S_{flow} = 1.1 \times \text{SMYS}\) as in earlier editions. The **modified B31G** method uses \(0.85\,d/t\) in place of \(\tfrac{2}{3}\,d/t\), a different \(M\), and \(S_{flow} = \text{SMYS} + 10\) ksi.
4. **Accept or reduce pressure.** The flaw is acceptable if \(S_F \ge SF \times S_O\), where \(S_O\) is the hoop stress at operating pressure and \(SF\) is a safety factor, recommended as the ratio of the hydrostatic test pressure to MAOP and usually not less than 1.25 (para. 1.9). If not, reduce the operating pressure below \(P_F/SF\), where \(P_F = 2 S_F t / D\).

!!! example "Is this pit acceptable?"
    NPS 20 (\(D\) = 20 in.), \(t\) = 0.375 in., pit depth \(d\) = 0.150 in. (40% of wall), corroded length \(L\) = 4 in., pipe operating at 72% of SMYS. Original B31G, Level 1.

    \(z = 4^2 / (20 \times 0.375) = 2.13\), so \(M = \sqrt{1 + 0.8 \times 2.13} \approx 1.645\).

    \[ S_F = 1.1\,\text{SMYS} \times \frac{1 - 0.267}{1 - 0.267/1.645} = 1.1\,\text{SMYS} \times \frac{0.733}{0.838} \approx 0.96\,\text{SMYS} \]

    \(S_F / S_O = 0.96 / 0.72 \approx 1.34\). That passes a safety factor of 1.25 but not 1.39, so whether it stays in service at this pressure depends on the factor the operator has set for this segment.

## Self-check

??? question "Name the three time-related threat types in B31.8S and one threat in each."
    Time-dependent (external corrosion), stable (defective pipe seam), time-independent (third-party damage).

??? question "What is the potential impact radius of a 36-in. gas line at 1,200 psig?"
    \(r = 0.69 \times 36 \times \sqrt{1{,}200} \approx 0.69 \times 36 \times 34.6 \approx 860\) ft.

??? question "A pit is 85% of wall thickness deep. Can you use the B31G tables?"
    No. The Level 0 tables do not evaluate metal loss deeper than 80% of nominal wall (section 3). A Level 1 or higher evaluation may, but the 2012 edition asks for due care with measurement accuracy and corrosion rate at that depth (para. 1.2(f)).

??? question "In a B31G Level 0 evaluation, what happens if the measured length is no longer than the table value?"
    The corroded area is acceptable; no pressure reduction is needed (para. 2.1).
