# B31.8S and B31G: Pipeline Integrity

Two companion standards that support the pipeline sections. Neither replaces B31.4 or B31.8; they tell an operator how to keep an existing line safe.

## B31.8S Managing System Integrity of Gas Pipelines

**Free PDF:** ASME B31.8S-2004 ([open PDF](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/cfr/ibr/002/asme.b31.8s.2004.pdf) · [archive.org page](https://archive.org/details/gov.law.asme.b31.8s.2004)), incorporated by reference in 49 CFR 192 Subpart O. Current edition: 2025 ([ASME](https://www.asme.org/codes-standards/find-codes-standards/b31-8s-managing-system-integrity-gas-pipelines)).

### Key ideas

**Threats.** Incident data were sorted into 22 root causes. Setting aside "unknown", B31.8S groups the remaining **21 threats** into **nine categories** under three time-related types (para. 2.2):

| Type | Categories |
| --- | --- |
| Time-dependent | External corrosion; internal corrosion; stress corrosion cracking |
| Stable | Manufacturing-related defects; welding and fabrication-related; equipment |
| Time-independent | Third-party or mechanical damage; incorrect operational procedure; weather-related and outside force |

Time-dependent threats grow, so they need periodic reassessment. Stable threats stay put unless something such as a pressure increase activates them. Time-independent threats can occur at any moment.

**Prescriptive or performance-based.** A prescriptive program follows the fixed data requirements and intervals in the standard (Appendix A, Table 3). A performance-based program uses more data and more detailed risk analysis to justify its own choices.

**Potential impact radius.** For natural gas, section 3 estimates the radius of the area a rupture could affect as

\[ r = 0.69 \, d \, \sqrt{p} \]

with \(r\) in feet, \(d\) the outside diameter in inches and \(p\) the maximum allowable operating pressure in psig. For a 30-in. line at 1,000 psig, \(r \approx 0.69 \times 30 \times 31.6 \approx 655\) ft.

**Three assessment methods.** In-line inspection ("pigging"), pressure testing and direct assessment (Appendix B). The method must suit the threat: a pressure test finds defects that fail at test pressure, while ILI tools find and size metal loss, dents or cracks depending on the tool.

## B31G Remaining Strength of Corroded Pipelines

**Free PDF:** ASME B31G-1991 ([open PDF](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/cfr/ibr/002/asme.b31g.1991.pdf) · [archive.org page](https://archive.org/details/gov.law.asme.b31g.1991)), incorporated by reference in 49 CFR 192 and 195. Current edition: 2023.

B31G answers one question: **can a corroded pipe stay in service at its current pressure, and if not, at what pressure?** It applies to pipe in B31.4, B31.8 and B31.11 systems.

### Key ideas

1. **Measure** the maximum pit depth \(d\) and the longitudinal extent \(L_m\) of the corroded area, and express depth as a percentage of nominal wall \(t\).
2. **Screen by depth.** 10% of wall or less: acceptable. More than 80%: repair or replace. Between the two, go to step 3.
3. **Allowable length (Part 2).**

    \[ L = 1.12 \, B \sqrt{D t}, \qquad B = \sqrt{\left(\frac{d/t}{1.1\,d/t - 0.15}\right)^2 - 1} \]

    with \(B \le 4\), and \(B = 4\) when depth is between 10% and 17.5%. If \(L_m \le L\), the area is acceptable.
4. **Safe pressure (Part 4).** If the area is longer than \(L\), compute

    \[ A = 0.893 \frac{L_m}{\sqrt{D t}} \]

    For \(A \le 4\): \( P' = 1.1 P \left[ \dfrac{1 - \tfrac{2}{3}(d/t)}{1 - \tfrac{2}{3}\,d/(t\sqrt{A^2+1})} \right] \). For \(A > 4\): \( P' = 1.1 P (1 - d/t) \). In both cases \(P' \le P\). If the operating pressure is above \(P'\), reduce it or repair.

    Here \(P\) is the greater of the established MAOP and the design pressure \(2StFT/D\).

!!! example "Is this pit acceptable?"
    NPS 20 (\(D\) = 20 in.), \(t\) = 0.375 in., pit depth \(d\) = 0.150 in. (40% of wall), corroded length \(L_m\) = 4 in.

    \(d/t = 0.40\), so \(B = \sqrt{(0.40 / (0.44 - 0.15))^2 - 1} = \sqrt{1.379^2 - 1} \approx 0.95\).

    \(L = 1.12 \times 0.95 \times \sqrt{20 \times 0.375} = 1.064 \times 2.739 \approx 2.9\) in.

    Since \(L_m\) = 4 in. is longer than 2.9 in., go to Part 4: \(A = 0.893 \times 4 / 2.739 \approx 1.30\), and

    \[ P' = 1.1P \frac{1 - 0.267}{1 - 0.267/\sqrt{1.30^2 + 1}} = 1.1P \frac{0.733}{1 - 0.163} \approx 0.96P \]

    So the segment may operate at up to about 96% of \(P\).

## Self-check

??? question "Name the three time-related threat types in B31.8S and one threat in each."
    Time-dependent (external corrosion), stable (defective pipe seam), time-independent (third-party damage).

??? question "What is the potential impact radius of a 36-in. gas line at 1,200 psig?"
    \(r = 0.69 \times 36 \times \sqrt{1{,}200} \approx 0.69 \times 36 \times 34.6 \approx 860\) ft.

??? question "A pit is 85% of wall thickness deep. What does B31G say?"
    Above 80% of wall, B31G does not allow the area to remain in service on its own calculation: repair or replace it.

??? question "In B31G Part 2, what happens if the measured length is shorter than L?"
    The corroded area is acceptable at the current MAOP; no pressure reduction is needed.
