# B31.3 and B31.12

**B31.3 Process Piping** has a free, legitimate PDF of the 2020 edition, which Public.Resource.Org posts because 33 CFR 127.003 incorporates it by reference. **B31.12 Hydrogen Piping and Pipelines** has none: to read it you need to buy it or use a library subscription.

## B31.3 Process Piping

**Free PDF:** ASME B31.3-2020 ([Internet Archive page](https://archive.org/details/pub.cfr.asme.b31.03.2020), 437 MB). It is too large for GitHub's 100 MB file limit, so this site links to it rather than hosting a copy, and the reading-map style page links are not available. Current edition: 2024, buy from [ASME](https://www.asme.org/codes-standards/find-codes-standards/b31-3-process-piping).

B31.3 is the most widely used section: refineries, chemical, pharmaceutical, textile, paper and pulp, semiconductor, cryogenic, hydrogen and power-generation plants, and related processing plants and terminals.

### Getting oriented

- **Learn the shared layout first.** B31.3 numbers its paragraphs in the 300s and follows the common chapter order: Chapter I scope (300), Chapter II design (301 to 322), III materials (323), IV standards (326), V fabrication (327 to 335), VI inspection, examination and testing (340 to 346). Once you know [the structure](structure.md) and have read [B31.1](b31-1.md), most of B31.3 Chapter II will look familiar.
- **Check the current table of contents** against the 2020 edition, for example the [B31.3-2022 ToC](https://www.asme.org/getmedia/2d6bec7b-1177-4ee1-82ea-e5d5a86abab7/B31-3_2022_ToC.pdf).
- **Use the [Interpretation Database](https://cstools.asme.org/Interpretation/SearchInterpretation.cfm).** B31.3 has more interpretations than any other section, and each one quotes the question and the paragraph.
- **Check a library.** University and company engineering libraries often hold ASME subscriptions.

### Concepts to know before you open it

| Concept | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Fluid service categories | B31.3 sorts fluids into Normal, Category D (nonflammable, nontoxic, low pressure and temperature), Category M (toxic, where a small exposure can cause serious harm), High Pressure (Chapter IX), and High Purity (Chapter X). The category drives examination and testing |
| Owner's responsibility | The owner decides the fluid service category and whether Category M or High Pressure rules apply |
| Leak test | Hydrostatic by default; the pneumatic option is lower and comes with extra precautions |
| Nonmetallic piping | Chapter VII covers plastics and other nonmetals |

These are summaries from general knowledge, not quotations; confirm each in the edition you use.

## B31.12 Hydrogen Piping and Pipelines

Current edition 2023, buy from [ASME](https://www.asme.org/Codes-Standards/Find-Codes-Standards/B31-12-Hydrogen-Piping-Pipelines). B31.12 covers gaseous and liquid hydrogen service in two parts: **Part IP** for industrial piping and **Part PL** for pipelines. ASME is moving the pipeline rules into a new chapter of B31.8, according to an [October 2024 ASME release](https://www.asme.org/about-asme/media-inquiries/press-releases/prci-provides-guidance-for-the-addition-of-hydrogen-pipeline-requirements-to-asme-b31-8-gas-transmission-and-distribution-piping-systems-standard).

ASME posts a free [B31.12-2023 table of contents](https://www.asme.org/getmedia/a31cab72-0492-4942-89a7-00a4eba1ee71/b31-12_2023_toc.pdf). For background on pipelines, [B31.8](b31-8.md) is the closest free book: B31.12 Part PL borrows its location-class approach.
