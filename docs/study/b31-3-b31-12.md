### Getting oriented

- **Learn the shared layout first.** B31.3 numbers its paragraphs in the 300s and follows the common chapter order. Once you know [the structure](structure.md) and have read [B31.1](b31-1.md), most of B31.3 Chapter II will look familiar.
- **Start with fluid service.** Appendix M walks through how to classify a fluid service, which decides which rules apply.
- **Use the [Interpretation Database](https://cstools.asme.org/Interpretation/SearchInterpretation.cfm).** B31.3 has more interpretations than any other section, and each one quotes the question and the paragraph.
- **Check the current edition** before design work; ASME publishes a free table of contents for each edition, for example the [B31.3-2022 ToC](https://www.asme.org/getmedia/2d6bec7b-1177-4ee1-82ea-e5d5a86abab7/B31-3_2022_ToC.pdf).

### Concepts to know before you open it

| Concept | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Fluid service categories | Normal; Category D (nonflammable, nontoxic, not damaging to human tissue, at no more than 150 psi gage and between −20°F and 366°F); Category M (so highly toxic that a single exposure to a very small quantity can cause serious irreversible harm); High Pressure (Chapter IX); and High Purity (Chapter X) (para. 300.2). The category drives examination and testing |
| Owner's responsibility | The owner has overall responsibility for compliance and decides the fluid service category, including whether Category M or High Pressure rules apply (para. 300(b)) |
| Leak test | Hydrostatic at no less than 1.5 × design pressure (345.4.2); the pneumatic alternative is at least 1.1 × design pressure and no more than 1.33 × (345.5.4), with extra precautions |
| Nonmetallic piping | Chapter VII covers plastics and other nonmetals |

These are summaries checked against the 2020 edition; confirm each in the edition you use.

## B31.12 Hydrogen Piping and Pipelines

Current edition 2023, buy from [ASME](https://www.asme.org/Codes-Standards/Find-Codes-Standards/B31-12-Hydrogen-Piping-Pipelines). B31.12 covers gaseous and liquid hydrogen service in two parts: **Part IP** for industrial piping and **Part PL** for pipelines. ASME is moving the pipeline rules into a new chapter of B31.8, according to an [October 2024 ASME release](https://www.asme.org/about-asme/media-inquiries/press-releases/prci-provides-guidance-for-the-addition-of-hydrogen-pipeline-requirements-to-asme-b31-8-gas-transmission-and-distribution-piping-systems-standard).

ASME posts a free [B31.12-2023 table of contents](https://www.asme.org/getmedia/a31cab72-0492-4942-89a7-00a4eba1ee71/b31-12_2023_toc.pdf). For background on pipelines, [B31.8](b31-8.md) is the closest free book: B31.12 Part PL borrows its location-class approach.
