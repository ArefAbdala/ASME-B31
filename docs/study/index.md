# Study plan

This plan takes you from "what is B31?" to reading a section with confidence. It uses only the free PDFs, so you can start today.

## How to use the study pages

Each section page has the same five parts:

1. **At a glance**: what the section is for and where its scope stops.
2. **Reading map**: the chapters in reading order, each with a link that opens the PDF at that page.
3. **Key ideas**: the concepts that carry the section, in plain language, with the paragraph to read.
4. **Worked numbers**: one or two short calculations that show how the rules are used.
5. **Self-check**: questions with hidden answers. Try them before you expand the answer.

## A suggested path

- [ ] **Week 1: The common skeleton.** Read [How the Code is organized](structure.md), then the Introduction and Chapter I of [B31.1-2016](b31-1.md). B31.1 is the oldest section, and the others copy its layout.
- [ ] **Week 2: Pick your section.** Use the [selection guide](../selection-guide.md) and [scenario quiz](scenarios.md) until you can place a system in the right book on the first try.
- [ ] **Week 3: Design.** Work through Chapter II of your section: design conditions, allowable stress, and the wall-thickness equation. Do the worked numbers by hand.
- [ ] **Week 4: Materials, fabrication and testing.** Chapters III to VI. Focus on welding, heat treatment, examination and the pressure test.
- [ ] **Week 5: Operation and integrity.** For pipelines, B31.4 or B31.8 Chapters on operation and corrosion, then [B31.8S and B31G](b31-8s-b31g.md).
- [ ] **Ongoing: The glossary.** Keep the [glossary](glossary.md) open as you read.

!!! tip "Large PDFs"
    B31.1, B31.3, B31.4 and B31.8 open on the Internet Archive and are 150 to 440 MB. Download each once and open it from your computer if the page jumps are slow.
