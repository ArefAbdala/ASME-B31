## At a glance

B31.4 covers pipelines that carry **liquids**: crude oil, condensate, natural gasoline, natural gas liquids, LPG, carbon dioxide, liquid alcohol, liquid anhydrous ammonia and petroleum products. It runs from production facilities and tank farms through pump stations and terminals, and the 2002 edition adds **offshore** pipelines in Chapter IX. Piping inside a refinery or process plant is B31.3, and gas lines are [B31.8](b31-8.md). Slurries were in the separate B31.11 when this edition was issued.

## Key ideas

**Design is based on yield strength.** Liquid pipelines are long, thin-walled and buried, so B31.4 limits hoop stress to a fraction of the pipe's specified minimum yield strength (SMYS). Para. 402.3.1 sets the allowable stress for pipe as

\[ S = 0.72 \times E \times \text{SMYS} \]

where \(E\) is the weld joint factor (1.0 for seamless and most ERW and SAW pipe).

**Wall thickness.** Para. 404.1.2 uses the Barlow thin-wall formula, \( t = P_i D / 2S \), then adds allowances for corrosion, threads and grooves to get the nominal wall.

**Hydrostatic test.** Para. 437.4.1 requires lines operating above 20% of SMYS to be proof tested to at least **1.25 × internal design pressure** for at least **4 hours**. Carbon dioxide systems must be hydrostatically tested (437.1.4).

**Operation matters as much as design.** Chapter VII covers the operator's O&M plan, right-of-way patrols, repair methods for dents, gouges and corrosion (451.6), and how to qualify a line for a higher pressure (456). Chapter VIII covers corrosion control.

## Worked numbers

!!! example "Wall thickness for a crude line"
    NPS 16 (\(D\) = 16.0 in.) API 5L X52 pipe (SMYS = 52,000 psi), seamless or ERW (\(E\) = 1.0), internal design pressure 1,200 psig.

    \[ S = 0.72 \times 1.0 \times 52{,}000 = 37{,}440 \text{ psi} \]
    \[ t = \frac{1{,}200 \times 16.0}{2 \times 37{,}440} \approx 0.256 \text{ in.} \]

    Add any corrosion allowance and choose the next standard wall above the result, for example 0.281 in. The hydrostatic test would be at least \(1.25 \times 1{,}200 = 1{,}500\) psig for 4 hours.

## Self-check

??? question "Does B31.4-2002 cover a CO₂ pipeline? A natural gas pipeline?"
    CO₂, yes: it is listed in the scope (para. 400.1) and must be hydrostatically tested. Natural gas, no: that is B31.8.

??? question "What fraction of SMYS sets the B31.4 allowable stress for pipe?"
    0.72 × E × SMYS (para. 402.3.1).

??? question "What hydrostatic test does a line operating above 20% SMYS need?"
    A proof test to at least 1.25 × internal design pressure for at least 4 hours (para. 437.4.1).

??? question "Where does B31.4 stop at a refinery?"
    At the plant boundary set by the scope diagrams (Fig. 400.1.1). Piping inside the refinery is B31.3.
