## At a glance

B31.4 covers pipelines that carry **liquids**: crude oil, condensate, natural gasoline, natural gas liquids, LPG, carbon dioxide, liquid alcohol, liquid anhydrous ammonia, petroleum products, produced and injection water, brine, biofuels and **slurries** (para. 400.1). It runs from production fields, tank farms and processing plants through pump stations and terminals to delivery points. The 2016 edition has separate chapters for **offshore** pipelines (IX), **carbon dioxide** (X) and **slurry** pipelines (XI). Piping inside a refinery or process plant is B31.3, and gas lines are [B31.8](b31-8.md).

## Key ideas

**Design is based on yield strength.** Liquid pipelines are long, thin-walled and buried, so B31.4 limits hoop stress to a fraction of the pipe's specified minimum yield strength (SMYS). Para. 403.2.1 sets the allowable stress for pipe as

\[ S = F \times E \times \text{SMYS} \]

where \(F\) is the design factor, not greater than 0.72, and \(E\) is the weld joint factor from Table 403.2.1-1 (1.0 for seamless and most ERW and SAW pipe). Table 403.3.1-1 sets the limits for the other stresses.

**Wall thickness.** Para. 403.2.1 uses the Barlow thin-wall formula, \( t = P_i D / 2S \), then adds allowances for corrosion, threads and grooves to get the nominal wall.

**Hydrostatic test.** Para. 437.4.1 requires lines operating above 20% of SMYS to be proof tested to at least **1.25 × internal design pressure** for at least **4 hours**. Every system must be tested after construction; lines at 20% of SMYS or less may use a leak test instead (437.1.4). Carbon dioxide lines must also be dewatered and dried before service (B437.4.1).

**Operation matters as much as design.** Chapter VII covers the operator's O&M plan, right-of-way patrols, integrity assessments and repairs of dents, gouges and corrosion (451.6), and how to qualify a line for a higher pressure (456). Chapter VIII covers corrosion control.

## Worked numbers

!!! example "Wall thickness for a crude line"
    NPS 16 (\(D\) = 16.0 in.) API 5L X52 pipe (SMYS = 52,000 psi), seamless or ERW (\(E\) = 1.0), internal design pressure 1,200 psig.

    \[ S = F \times E \times \text{SMYS} = 0.72 \times 1.0 \times 52{,}000 = 37{,}440 \text{ psi} \]
    \[ t = \frac{1{,}200 \times 16.0}{2 \times 37{,}440} \approx 0.256 \text{ in.} \]

    Add any corrosion allowance and choose the next standard wall above the result, for example 0.281 in. The hydrostatic test would be at least \(1.25 \times 1{,}200 = 1{,}500\) psig for 4 hours.

## Self-check

??? question "Does B31.4-2016 cover a CO₂ pipeline? A natural gas pipeline?"
    CO₂, yes: it is in the scope (para. 400.1) and Chapter X adds rules for it. Natural gas, no: that is B31.8.

??? question "What fraction of SMYS sets the B31.4 allowable stress for pipe?"
    F × E × SMYS, with the design factor F no greater than 0.72 (para. 403.2.1).

??? question "What hydrostatic test does a line operating above 20% SMYS need?"
    A proof test to at least 1.25 × internal design pressure for at least 4 hours (para. 437.4.1).

??? question "Where does B31.4 stop at a refinery?"
    At the plant boundary set by the scope diagrams (Fig. 400.1.1-1). Piping inside the refinery is B31.3.
