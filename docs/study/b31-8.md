## At a glance

B31.8 covers gas **transmission** and **gathering** pipelines, compressor stations, metering and regulating stations, and gas **distribution** mains and service lines up to the outlet of the customer's meter set assembly (para. 802.1). It also has chapters on offshore lines (VIII) and sour gas (IX). Liquid lines are [B31.4](b31-4.md); gas processing plant piping is B31.3. For integrity management and corroded pipe, see [B31.8S and B31G](b31-8s-b31g.md).

## Key ideas

**Location class sets the stress level.** The more people live near a line, the lower the stress it may run at. Para. 840.2 lays a zone ¼ mile wide centered on the pipeline, divides it into 1-mile sections, and counts buildings intended for human occupancy (each dwelling unit counts separately):

| Location class | Buildings in any 1-mile section | Basic design factor F (Table 841.1.6-1) | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1, Division 1 | 10 or fewer | 0.80 | F above 0.72, up to 0.80; water test only |
| 1, Division 2 | 10 or fewer | 0.72 | F 0.72 or less; water, air or gas test |
| 2 | 11 to 45 | 0.60 | Fringe areas, industrial areas, country estates |
| 3 | 46 or more | 0.50 | Suburban housing, shopping centers |
| 4 | Multistory buildings (4+ floors) prevalent | 0.40 | Heavy traffic, many underground utilities |

Places of public assembly such as schools, hospitals and churches in Class 1 or 2 areas must meet Class 3 requirements (840.3).

**The design formula.** Para. 841.1.1 gives the design pressure of steel pipe as

\[ P = \frac{2 S t}{D} \times F \times E \times T \]

with \(S\) the SMYS, \(t\) the nominal wall, \(D\) the outside diameter, \(F\) the design factor, \(E\) the longitudinal joint factor (Table 841.1.7-1) and \(T\) the temperature derating factor (Table 841.1.8-1, 1.000 at 250°F and below). Table 841.1.6-2 lowers F for crossings, fabricated assemblies and compressor stations.

**Testing.** After construction each line is strength tested and leak tested (841.3). The test pressure, test medium and resulting maximum allowable operating pressure depend on location class (Table 841.3.2-1): for lines at 30% of SMYS or more, at least 1.25 × MOP in Location Classes 1 and 2 and 1.5 × MOP in Classes 3 and 4.

**Location classes change.** When building counts rise along an existing line, para. 854 tells the operator how to confirm or reduce the operating pressure, or replace pipe.

## Worked numbers

!!! example "Design pressure of a transmission line"
    NPS 24 (\(D\) = 24.0 in.), API 5L X60 (\(S\) = 60,000 psi), wall 0.375 in., Location Class 2 (\(F\) = 0.60), \(E\) = 1.0, \(T\) = 1.0.

    \[ P = \frac{2 \times 60{,}000 \times 0.375}{24.0} \times 0.60 \times 1.0 \times 1.0 = 1{,}875 \times 0.60 = 1{,}125 \text{ psig} \]

    The same pipe in Class 3 (\(F\) = 0.50) is limited to 937 psig, and in Class 1 Division 2 (\(F\) = 0.72) to 1,350 psig.

## Self-check

??? question "A 1-mile section has 30 houses within the ¼-mile zone. Which location class?"
    Location Class 2 (more than 10 but fewer than 46 buildings).

??? question "What does each letter in P = (2St/D) × F × E × T mean?"
    S, specified minimum yield strength; t, nominal wall thickness; D, outside diameter; F, design factor from location class; E, longitudinal joint factor; T, temperature derating factor (para. 841.1.1).

??? question "A Class 1 line has a school beside it. What changes?"
    Pipelines near places of public assembly in Class 1 or 2 must meet Location Class 3 requirements (para. 840.3), unless the facility is used infrequently.

??? question "Where does B31.8 end at a customer's premises?"
    At the outlet of the customer's meter set assembly. Piping inside the building is fuel gas piping under the National Fuel Gas Code (NFPA 54 / ANSI Z223.1).
