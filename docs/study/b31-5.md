# B31.5 Refrigeration Piping

**Free PDF:** ASME B31.5-2016, *Refrigeration Piping and Heat Transfer Components* ([open PDF](../pdfs/asme.b31.05.2016.pdf), 91 MB · [archive.org page](https://archive.org/details/pub.cfr.asme.b31.05.2016)). Current edition: 2022 ([ASME](https://www.asme.org/codes-standards/find-codes-standards/b31-5-refrigeration-piping-heat-transfer-components)).

## At a glance

B31.5 covers **refrigerant, heat transfer component and secondary coolant piping** for temperatures as low as **−320°F (−196°C)**, whether it is built on site or assembled in a factory (para. 500.1.1). It does **not** apply to (para. 500.1.3):

- self-contained or unit systems covered by Underwriters Laboratories or another recognized testing laboratory,
- water piping, other than where water is used as a secondary coolant or refrigerant,
- piping designed for 15 psi gage or less, internal or external, regardless of size,
- pressure vessels, compressors or pumps (but it does cover the connecting piping from the first joint).

Chilled water loops that serve a building are normally [B31.9](b31-9.md), which defines chilled water and covers it within its limits.

## Key ideas

**Design pressure comes from the refrigerant.** Para. 501.2.4 sets a minimum design pressure of 15 psig and, beyond that, the refrigerant's saturation pressure at a reference temperature that depends on which side of the system the piping is on:

| Part of system | Reference temperature |
| --- | --- |
| Low side, all systems | 80°F (27°C) |
| High side, water or evaporatively cooled | 104°F (40°C) |
| High side, air cooled | 122°F (50°C) |

Look up the refrigerant's saturation pressure at that temperature, and that is your minimum design pressure.

**Trapped liquid.** Liquid refrigerant trapped between closed valves expands as it warms and can burst the pipe, so the design must provide a means to prevent overpressure (501.4.2).

**Brittle fracture.** At low temperature many steels lose toughness. Para. 501.3.1 and Chapter III (523.2) require you to consider it, which drives material choice and impact testing.

**Field pressure test.** Para. 538.4.2 tests refrigerant piping with gas: an optional preliminary test at 25 psig finds big leaks, then the test pressure is at least **110% of design pressure** and no more than 130% of the design pressure of any component.

## Self-check

??? question "Does B31.5 cover a glycol loop at 10 psig?"
    No. Piping designed for 15 psi or less is excluded (para. 500.1.3(c)).

??? question "What minimum design pressure applies to the high side of an air-cooled R-134a system?"
    The saturation pressure of R-134a at 122°F (50°C), and not less than 15 psig (para. 501.2.4). For R-134a that is about 175 psig; confirm it in a refrigerant property table.

??? question "Why does B31.5 worry about liquid trapped between valves?"
    Because a liquid-full section that warms up builds pressure quickly; the design must include a relief path or other means to prevent overpressure (501.4.2).

??? question "What is the minimum pneumatic field test pressure for refrigerant piping?"
    110% of the design pressure, not exceeding 130% of the design pressure of any component (para. 538.4.2(e)).
