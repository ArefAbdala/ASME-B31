# B31.1 Power Piping

**Free PDF:** ASME B31.1-2007 ([open PDF](https://archive.org/download/gov.law.asme.b31.1.2007/asme.b31.1.2007.pdf), 26 MB · [archive.org page](https://archive.org/details/gov.law.asme.b31.1.2007)), with addenda [B31.1a-2008](https://archive.org/details/gov.law.asme.b31.1a.2008) and [B31.1b-2009](https://archive.org/details/gov.law.asme.b31.1b.2009). Current edition: 2026 ([ASME](https://www.asme.org/codes-standards/find-codes-standards/b31-1-power-piping)).

## At a glance

B31.1 covers piping in **electric power stations, industrial and institutional plants, geothermal heating systems, and central and district heating and cooling systems**. Its best-known job is **boiler external piping (BEP)**: the piping between a power boiler and its first stop valves, which B31.1 governs jointly with Section I of the ASME Boiler and Pressure Vessel Code. If a building system stays inside B31.9's limits, use [B31.9](b31-9.md) instead; process plants belong to B31.3.

## Key ideas

**Boiler external piping is special.** Fig. 100.1.2(A) and (B) draw the line between the boiler (Section I), BEP (B31.1 rules but Section I jurisdiction, with an Authorized Inspector) and non-boiler external piping (B31.1 alone). Para. 122.1 sets the extra BEP design rules, and para. 137.3.1 sends BEP to a Section I hydrostatic test (PG-99) witnessed by the Authorized Inspector.

**Allowable stress depends on temperature.** Para. 102.3 points to the stress tables in Appendix A, where each material has a column of allowable stress values that fall as temperature rises. At high temperature the value is set by creep, which is why power piping alloys (P91, P22) matter.

**Minimum wall thickness.** Para. 104.1.2(A) gives the pressure design thickness of straight pipe from the design pressure, outside diameter, allowable stress, a weld joint efficiency and a temperature coefficient \(y\), then adds allowances for threads, grooves, corrosion and erosion. The form is

\[ t_m = \frac{P D_o}{2(SE + P y)} + A \]

and you then add the mill under-tolerance (12.5% for most seamless pipe) before choosing a schedule (12.5% applies to most seamless pipe bought to ASTM A106 or A53; check your pipe specification).

**Flexibility.** Hot piping grows. Para. 119 asks whether the system is flexible enough that the displacement stress range stays within the allowable range, using the stress intensification factors in Appendix D.

**Testing.** Para. 137.4.5 requires a hydrostatic test of at least **1.5 × design pressure**, held for at least **10 minutes**, then lowered to design pressure for the leak examination.

**Covered piping systems.** Chapter VII, added in the 2007 edition, asks owners of high-energy "covered piping systems" (CPS) to keep operation and maintenance procedures, assess the condition of the piping, and keep records.

## Worked numbers

!!! example "Wall thickness for a steam line"
    A seamless carbon steel line, NPS 8 (\(D_o\) = 8.625 in.), design pressure 600 psig. Suppose the Appendix A allowable stress at design temperature is \(S\) = 15,000 psi, with \(E\) = 1.0 (seamless), \(y\) = 0.4 and no allowances (\(A\) = 0).

    \[ t_m = \frac{600 \times 8.625}{2(15{,}000 \times 1.0 + 600 \times 0.4)} = \frac{5{,}175}{30{,}480} \approx 0.170 \text{ in.} \]

    Adding 12.5% mill tolerance: \(0.170 / 0.875 \approx 0.194\) in. Schedule 40 NPS 8 has a nominal wall of 0.322 in., so it is adequate. Always read \(S\) from the stress table for your material and temperature; 15,000 psi is an illustration.

## Self-check

??? question "Who has jurisdiction over boiler external piping, and which Code supplies the rules?"
    ASME Section I has jurisdiction (the piping needs an Authorized Inspector and Section I documentation), while the technical rules for design, materials, fabrication and testing come from B31.1. See Fig. 100.1.2 and para. 122.1.

??? question "What is the minimum hydrostatic test pressure and hold time in B31.1-2007?"
    1.5 × design pressure, held at least 10 minutes, then reduced to design pressure for examination (para. 137.4.5).

??? question "A district heating plant serves a university campus. B31.1 or B31.9?"
    B31.1. Central and district heating plants are in B31.1's scope, and B31.9 is for piping within a building's property limits and within its pressure and temperature limits.

??? question "What does the \(y\) coefficient in the wall-thickness equation account for?"
    It adjusts the thin-wall formula for material and temperature. It is 0.4 for ferritic and austenitic steels below the creep range and rises at higher temperatures for ferritic steels (Table 104.1.2(A), Values of y).

??? question "Where do you look for the materials B31.1 allows and their allowable stresses?"
    Chapter III (para. 123) for the rules, and Mandatory Appendix A for the stress tables.
