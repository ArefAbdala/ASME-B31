# Which section? Scenario quiz

For each system, decide which B31 section applies and which free PDF you would open. Then expand the answer. The reasoning follows the [selection guide](../selection-guide.md); a law, a regulator or the owner's specification always overrides it.

??? question "1. Main steam from a utility boiler to the turbine in a coal-fired power station."
    **B31.1** ([study page](b31-1.md)). The piping from the boiler to its first stop valve is boiler external piping under Section I jurisdiction; beyond it, B31.1 alone.

??? question "2. A 30-mile, 20-in. crude oil line from a tank farm to a refinery."
    **B31.4** up to the refinery boundary ([study page](b31-4.md)). Inside the refinery, B31.3 ([free 2020 edition](https://archive.org/details/pub.cfr.asme.b31.03.2020)).

??? question "3. Natural gas service line from the street main to a house meter."
    **B31.8** ([study page](b31-8.md)). B31.8 covers distribution mains and service lines up to the outlet of the customer's meter set assembly. Beyond the meter, the National Fuel Gas Code.

??? question "4. Chilled water at 45°F and 125 psig circulating through an office tower."
    **B31.9** ([study page](b31-9.md)). Water loops in a building are within B31.9's limits; water piping is excluded from B31.5.

??? question "5. Ammonia refrigerant piping between the compressors and evaporators of a cold-storage warehouse."
    **B31.5** ([study page](b31-5.md)). Refrigerant piping above 15 psig.

??? question "6. Piping that moves sulfuric acid between tanks in a chemical plant."
    **B31.3** Process Piping. Read the free [2020 edition](https://archive.org/details/pub.cfr.asme.b31.03.2020); see [B31.3 and B31.12](b31-3-b31-12.md).

??? question "7. Hot water heating at 300°F and 200 psig in a hospital."
    **B31.1**. B31.9 limits nonflammable liquids to 250°F, so 300°F is outside it.

??? question "8. A CO₂ pipeline carrying dense-phase carbon dioxide for enhanced oil recovery."
    **B31.4**. Carbon dioxide is in B31.4's scope (para. 400.1), and B31.4 requires a hydrostatic test for CO₂ lines.

??? question "9. A 36-in. gas transmission line where a new housing estate has gone up beside it."
    **B31.8** for the line, with para. 854 for a change in location class, and **B31.8S** for the integrity program ([study page](b31-8s-b31g.md)).

??? question "10. An in-line inspection finds a corroded patch 30% deep on a liquid line. Can it stay in service?"
    Use **B31G** ([study page](b31-8s-b31g.md)): check the allowable length in Part 2 and, if the patch is longer, the safe pressure in Part 4.

??? question "11. Hydrogen piping at a refueling station."
    **B31.12** (Part IP), which has no free PDF; see [B31.3 and B31.12](b31-3-b31-12.md).

??? question "12. Compressed air at 100 psig in a factory building."
    **B31.9** if it is building service piping within the limits (compressed air to 150 psig). If the air is part of a process plant's process, B31.3 is often specified instead.

??? question "13. A central plant supplying steam at 125 psig to six buildings on a campus."
    **B31.1**. A central or district plant serving several buildings is in B31.1's scope, even below B31.9's pressure limit.

??? question "14. Fuel gas piping from the meter to the boilers inside a hotel."
    Neither B31.8 nor B31.9 in most cases: building fuel gas piping is under the National Fuel Gas Code (NFPA 54 / ANSI Z223.1). B31.2, the old B31 fuel gas section, is withdrawn.
