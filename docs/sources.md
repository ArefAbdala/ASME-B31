## Free, legitimate access

There is no free read-only copy of the current editions. What does exist:

- **Tables of contents.** ASME posts free ToC PDFs for most sections, for example [B31.3-2022](https://www.asme.org/getmedia/2d6bec7b-1177-4ee1-82ea-e5d5a86abab7/B31-3_2022_ToC.pdf), [B31.8-2022](https://www.asme.org/getmedia/72fd7ce5-8181-4234-a1e4-520ce82d7eb3/b31-8_2022_toc.pdf), [B31.12-2023](https://www.asme.org/getmedia/a31cab72-0492-4942-89a7-00a4eba1ee71/b31-12_2023_toc.pdf) and [B31G-2023](https://www.asme.org/getmedia/6587a6cf-437c-4426-8d33-0ff00a5bbdbc/b31g_2023_toc.pdf).
- **Interpretations.** ASME's [Interpretation Database](https://cstools.asme.org/Interpretation/SearchInterpretation.cfm) is free to search and shows how committees have read specific paragraphs.
- **Editions written into US pipeline law.** [49 CFR 195.3](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/subtitle-B/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-195/subpart-A/section-195.3) incorporates B31.4-2019, B31.8-2018 and B31G-1991 (R2004). These can be inspected at PHMSA or the National Archives, and [PHMSA's incorporated-by-reference page](https://www.phmsa.dot.gov/standards-rulemaking/pipeline/standards-incorporated-reference) offers temporary access on request by email (reply within 5 business days). They are older editions than those for sale.
- **Libraries.** University and company libraries often hold ASME standards subscriptions; check your institution's engineering library.
